## Pacing and ownership

Candidate production identity: c3e6b02d198bbcdad46d403101706bbe859390d72729c6391adb01751ba0c3ca, 90 files. The author's frozen nine-path manifest is SHA-256 d3e019eecf11462b048a09ccd1f8eaece720a35feda91d8eae938259a670799a. Only seven application source/style paths differ from the original baseline; the two additional paths are tests.

The reviewed app adapter admits an entire R3F demand tick only after the prior actual WebGL2 submission prefix completes. It does not skip renderer calls, fake completion, impose a lower fixed refresh ceiling, reduce render quality, or alter the native input/capture script. Completion is queried nonblocking. Outside-RAF environment renders are forwarded intact and fenced. Pending invalidations coalesce and are retained for the next admissible tick. Cleanup cancels owner timers/effects, restores ownership and deletes the retained fence. Native failure and context loss remain distinct from a valid pending operation.

After five seconds of valid continuous pending work, the scene presents a nonfatal waiting notice with a manual Retry viewer action. This feedback threshold is not a failure deadline. Retry retires the old Canvas/owner and advances a monotonic scene generation. Completion, failure and owner cleanup clear the status; retired-owner callbacks cannot change replacement status. Warm route initialization must not mount and immediately retire an extra Canvas.

The adapter intentionally depends on the installed R3F loop/numeric-frame-counter behavior; its installed-loop and call-site tests are required. Existing camera/appearance interpolation delta caps are unchanged, so under slow completion they may lengthen wall-clock convergence. No speed or smoothness claim follows from passing synthetic scheduling tests.

## Independent acceptance lanes

The new closed request uses render-backpressure-2m-msaa-v1 and the exact reviewed production source identity. It requires both [stage-dpr-motion] and [stage-cabin-integration] on a main push. Historical source contracts cannot substitute for this candidate.

The original normal-motion lane retains the byte-identical native driver and observer: every original phase and 13 ordered nonblank captures, desktop/mobile and deliberate resize rows, native input, two maximum 45-second screenshot attempts, MSAA creation chronology, unchanged buffer/context/resource/error/flicker/performance assertions and original model response identities.

A separate required desktop/mobile lifecycle job executes whole-tick, notice, generation, warm-navigation and disposal tests plus an actual-browser lifecycle script. A controlled harness-only fixture delays observation of native positive completion for an explicitly identified scene context; it records native and substituted statuses and never turns a failure or loss into success. The fixture is released in the trusted native Retry click's capture phase before the app handles the click, so old-context disposal and the new context observe real completion. The lane must demonstrate status-only completion without a remount, manual retry with a real new context, silence of retired fence owners, restored model/window readiness, settled image decoding, bitmap closure and live-context texture deletion. This is injected-delay ownership evidence, not a throughput or hardware-performance result.

Source identity is checked before candidate setup and again before browser/server creation. All production bundle and model bodies remain hash-bound. The lifecycle job cannot replace or suppress full native-motion acceptance. Authorized CI is the only environment allowed to execute these browser/server scenarios; local validation is socket-free.
